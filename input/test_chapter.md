It was like a scene straight out of a book, Raiel noted. Here he was, surrounded by his tired and battered team, in a desperate fight against their final boss. Of course, it had to be raining. And cold.  

Dark clouds rolled ominously overhead, swirling in a way that seemed almost unnatural, as if even the sky knew the gravity of what was unfolding. The ground beneath his feet was slick with rain, making every movement a struggle. His clothes clung uncomfortably to his damp skin, and the knife in his hand seemed to grow heavier with each passing moment as his strained muscles faltered. The rest of his gear was gone, strewn across the hollowed-out streets he had called home for all 20 years of his life. This place, it wasn’t real. Not technically. His handgun lay useless across the street, and his armor hung from a light pole behind him. He knew he could reach them in a heartbeat if he wanted to, but this fight had been going on for hours, and Raiel didn’t know if he could even move. 

He could feel something else, a presence that lingered just outside the realm of his perception. Elysium—the very dimension they were fighting in—was distorting and shifting around that thing. Swirls of darkness darkening even further as if it too could sense the power it held. It was a reflection of their souls, of their emotions. This was Elysium—a world of perception, and it was shifting, changing, as if reacting to the impending doom. The ground trembled beneath Raiel’s feet as if the dimension itself could no longer hold the weight of their conflict. It groaned under the pressure, distorting like a cracked mirror. Jagged shards of reality hung in the air, suspended in time, flickering in and out of existence. Every breath Raiel took felt heavy, like the air around him was suddenly gone.  

Raiel’s heart skipped a beat as he saw a figure emerge from the swirling mass of darkness, stepping forward with an unsettling calm. His final boss was here. Its form was barely visible, a silhouette composed of shadows and flickering fragments of light. The edges of its figure seemed to bleed into the surrounding chaos, as if it didn’t belong here, or anywhere for that matter. Its face, if you could call it that, was a shifting void, a black hole where features should have been, sucking in the light around it. The presence of this unnatural, unreal being was cracking the fabric of Elysium. The human perception can only handle so much wrongness before it becomes unstable.  

A low growl escaped from the void where its mouth should have been, a sound that reverberated through Elysium like a death knell. The figure raised a hand. Long, skeletal fingers stretched out toward Raiel. The very air shimmered and warped in response.  

And then, it spoke. 

"Raiel." 

The voice wasn't like anything he'd heard before. It was the sound of a thousand whispers layered over each other, broken and fragmented, as though coming from both inside and outside his mind. It was cold, detached, and it made his stomach churn. 

"You've been a thorn in my side for too long.” The figure's voice carried with it a tinge of excitement, though no expression could truly form on its dark face. “But here... in Elysium, all things must end. You will not escape this time. Not like before. You will die here." 

Raiel stumbled back, the weight of the figure's presence crashing over him. He wanted to raise his knife, wanted to fight back, but his body refused to obey. His muscles screamed in protest, and his grip tightened weakly around the weapon. He could feel the energy of Elysium pulsing around him, but it wasn’t the comforting hum he’d felt earlier when everything was still. This felt wrong. The dimension was dying along with them. 

Thing’s hand reached out farther. Impossibly long fingers twitched with an unsettling precision. Raiel’s vision swam. A sudden wave of vertigo hit him, and his legs buckled. His knees hit the ground, but he couldn’t focus on the pain. All he could see was the encroaching darkness, the tendrils of the entity’s reach expanding with every heartbeat. 

"Raiel...” The figure’s voice was sharper now, a biting edge of anticipation threading through the words. “Do you understand? You are mine. Everything you’ve done... it has been for nothing." 

Raiel’s vision flickered again.  

With what little strength remained, Raiel forced himself to his feet, fighting against the gravity of both Elysium and his own body. He gritted his teeth, the last of his willpower screaming to life as his Eidolon flared to life—a flash of azure light cutting through the shadows. 

He hoped it would be enough. 

His hand, trembling, reached for the knife once more. Quick as lightning, the figure’s fingers brushed his skin. Its fingers almost teleporting through the mist that hung in the air. With the last of his effort, Raiel sunk his knife into the thing's flesh. It didn’t even so much as flinch. Suddenly large fingers encapsulated his vision and Raiel was plunged into darkness. He could hear his Eidolon ripping at its flesh, and it seemed like his teammate’s eidolons had joined the fray. Raiel struggled against the grip. 

 In a sudden realization, he noticed that he couldn’t feel his body. The handle of his knife, his soaked clothes, his protesting muscles, it was all gone. 

After what felt like an eternity, there was a definite thunk from somewhere, and he realized that he could feel again. Waves of pain and the screams of his protesting limbs hit him all at once, and he suddenly found that he very much liked not feeling.  

Then it all ended just as soon as it had arrived. 

Raiel’s world cracked wide open. Elysium shattered, and the final boss’ presence, though ever oppressive, faltered. For just a moment, the balance tipped. 

Then everything went black. 

And Raiel fell into the void.
